BSc Hons Computing
1 Introduction
We have seen the continued growth and adaption of digital system with the
continuous need for intelligent interactive computing devices. Whether these are for
entertainment, education, work, health care or transport, computing devices have
become an intrinsic part of everyday life.
Given the breadth of the subject domain and its ever-changing nature, graduates of the
BSc Computing course are well positioned for a range of employment opportunities
including software development, database/systems administration, artificial
intelligence systems, mobile application development, web development and user-
centred design to name a few. The BSc Computing degree is designed to produce
graduates that have a high level of knowledge across a variety of computing
technologies. Students will build on the fundamental of computing by learning
practical skills such as advanced programming, user centred design, web development
and mobile application development.
The course will be taught jointly between Coventry University and NIBM. It will be
based at NIBM who is one of the leading computing-training institute in Sri Lanka
with a long history, which provides well-recognised IT qualifications and training in
Sri Lanka.
Innovation
The course is organised and staged to provide appropriate structures, support and
challenges within modules and across the course. Level 5 of the course is designed
to build on the HND courses that the students have studied before starting the BSc
Computing. Level 6 takes advantages of the knowledge and skills learned in Level 5,
to incorporate advanced concepts. The two project modules in Level 6 allow the
students to combine the knowledge and skills developed, with the opportunity to take
up a project that truly benefits the Sri Lanka community that the students live in.
The BSc Computing course integrates employability at the centre through the creation
of a range of professional skills: problem solving, initiative self and resource
management; group working; effective communications and process management.

AIMS
The aims of the BSc Computing programme are,
• Create graduates who are computing specialists with skills in areas such as
systems analysis and design, programming, web development, databases,
artificial intelligence, mobile application development and user centred design.
Such skills should be applicable to a wider range of industries and destinations
who make use of computing. This includes practical abilities in particular the
ability to write code in a high-level programming language but also the use of
appropriate software and technology such as database systems and version
control systems.
• Develop transferable skills such as problem solving, critical analysis, clear
communication, and teamwork.
• Develop content and practices that prepares students for software development
and application to benefit society through understanding of the social, ethical,
legal, and professional aspects of a cooperative human environment such as
the workplace.
• Acquire additional skills, such as independent and creative thinking, to pursue
further studies and research.
Course Learning Outcomes
On successful completion of the course a student will be able to,
1. Understand and apply the underlying technology, design methods and
programming languages required to practice in the real-world.
2. Understand professional practices of the modern IT industry which include
those technical (e.g. version control / automated testing) but also social, ethical
& legal responsibilities.
3. Create secure artefact that combine diverse technologies and techniques for a
computing domain that meets a set of agreed requirements.
4. Understand and apply emergent technologies and techniques in the wider
computing domain.
5. Conduct in depth research and apply appropriate design and problem-solving
techniques to computing and solve real-world problems.

6. Work with large datasets use appropriate storage technology to apply analysis
techniques including machine learning tools and artificial intelligence.

AGILE
MODULE SUMMARY
Aims and Summary
This module aims to provide students with sound understanding and experience in
Development methodologies, techniques, and tools. Agile and rapid approaches to
developing information systems and software are critical for responding to changing
business environment. The module will examine in detail different methodologies for
rapid development and their application in practice. Specific industry strength type of
technology for Rapid Application Development (RAD) will be utilised for solving real-
world problems.

Learning credits 20 credits

TEACHING, LEARNING AND ASSESSMENT
Intended Module Learning Outcomes
On successful completion of this module, the learner should be able to:
1. Demonstrate a sound understanding of how Agile Methodologies can be used
to define users’ requirements, analysis and design of information systems.
2. Compare and contrast a range of current and emerging agile methodologies.
3. Evaluate the methods, techniques and tools for rapid development of various
types of information systems, and the reasons for their selection and use.
4. Use a range of appropriate tools to contribute to the development of a
solution to a real-world problem.
Indicative Content
• Overview of Agile Methodologies; Agile development model; and phases of
development. Major agile methodologies; strengths and weaknesses.
• Agile approaches: Dynamic Systems Development Method, Extreme
Programming, Scrum, Kanban, Lean Software Development, Pair
Programming.
• Scaling Agile for building large and complex solutions.
• Distributed Version Control: forking, merge requests, coding standards,
branching, merging, code reviews.
• Automated Testing: unit testing, UI testing, acceptance testing, regression
testing, continuous development, continuous deployment.
• Problems and Challenges including technical, ethical and security concerns
related to Agile.

USER EXPERIENCE DESIGNING
MODULE SUMMARY
Aims and Summary
This module is an advanced level Human Computer Interaction course, which
teaches the skills needed to become an excellent HCI practitioner approaching
professional competence.
The module is organised around an approach called User Centred Design (UCD), an
iterative process which integrates user studies, interface design, prototypes and user
evaluation to produce design concepts that match the requirements of users.
UCD can be implemented in many ways. To support this, the module presents a
range of qualitative and quantitative approaches to user data capture analysis,
looking at a diverse range of methods from ethnographically informed observation to
formal usability evaluation.
The module will consider a range of key issues, including social and cultural factors
that influence the way individuals and groups interact with computers and
communicate when using computer-mediated systems. The module will look at
applications of computing to work, learning, and leisure (including games)
considering what usability means and entails in these different domains. Different
computing architectures are explored to establish the impact on usability evaluation
and design.

4. Explain, select and apply appropriate methodology to given design problems,

Usability Studies

Interaction, Collaboration and Communication

Interface Design
User interface design. User, task, and interaction modelling. User monitoring.
Behavioural and social design and use.
Prototyping and Evaluation
Conceptual prototyping tools and techniques: paper and digital prototypes. Lo-, mid-
and hi-fidelity prototyping. Horizontal and vertical prototyping.

COMPUTER VISION
MODULE SUMMARY
Aims and Summary
Machine Vision is concerned with the capture of an image, its presentation,
enhancement and analysis to permit decision making. This module introduces the
models, algorithms and hardware involved, providing practical experience with image
data. Sufficient mathematics is introduced within the module to allow students to
appreciate the background to the methods being studied but emphasis is placed on
the selection and use of appropriate techniques in the context of application areas
such as G.I.S., Medical Image Processing, and Machine Vision in Manufacturing.
Module Size and credits
Module level 6

Assessment credits 20 credits

Total student study hours 200 hours
Total study weeks 13
Pre-requisites and co-requisites None
TEACHING, LEARNING AND ASSESSMENT
Intended Module Learning Outcomes
On successful completion of this module, the learner should be able to:
1. Evaluate the suitability of image models and representations commonly used
in image and computer vision.
2. Critically discuss a variety of hardware and software technologies used in
acquisition, display and transmission of image data.
3. Select, apply and critically evaluate a variety of algorithms typically used in
the processing and analysis of images, including techniques for filtering,
segmentation, labelling and feature extraction.
4. Evaluate the suitability of concepts, representations and techniques used in
object and pattern recognition.

Aims and Summary

intelligence is and what its aims are from Alan Turing’s famous human-computer

well as design considerations for implementing such systems. It draws on research
