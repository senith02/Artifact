1. Introduction

1.1. Background

The  modern  software  industry  heavily  relies  on  Continuous  Integration  and

Continuous  Deployment  (CI/CD)  pipelines  to  release  software  faster  and  more

reliably.  These  pipelines  enable  developers  to  automate  workflows  across  the

building, testing, and deployment stages of development. Some platforms provide

cloud environments to run pipelines and automate workflows, rather than requiring

teams  to  manage  their  own  infrastructure.  GitHub  Actions,  GitLab  CI,  and

Bitbucket Pipelines are examples of platforms that support these workloads.

While  CI/CD  pipelines  help  developers  to  improve  their  productivity  it  also

consumes  significant  computing  resources  such  as  CPU,  memory  and  network

bandwidth. These resources indirectly contribute to energy consumption and carbon

emissions in data centers. The rapid growth of software industry including AI data

centers raised up the amount of environmental impact, positioning this challenge at

the forefront of Green Computing and Sustainable Software Industry.

However,  most  developers  and  organizations  have  limited  visibility  into  the

environmental  impact  of  their  development  workflows.  Most  developers  mainly

consider  performance,  reliability  and  scalability  of  their  workflows  rather  than

environmental sustainability. Because of that developers rarely consider how their

pipeline configuration affects energy consumption and carbon emissions.

This research aims to explore the carbon footprint and energy consumption of the

CI/CD pipelines across popular and mostly used DevOps platforms and evaluate

how pipeline designs may affect the environmental impact.

While  these  platforms  offers  cloud  hosted  execution  environments  still  the

underlying  hardware  configurations  are  remain  unknown  and  vary  between

platforms  to  platforms.  This  makes  direct  energy  comparisons  scientifically

unreliable.  This  research  addresses  this  issue  by  using  self  hosted  runners  on  a

standardized virtual machine. So we can ensure hardware is the controlled constant

rather than a variable.

1.2. Research Problem

Although CI/CD pipelines are widely used in modern software development, the

environmental  impact  of  these  automated  workflows  is  rarely  considered.

Developers  often  rely  on  hosted  services  without  considering  the  energy  use  or

carbon emissions generated by pipeline runs.

DevOps tools that are currently used in the industry provide detailed metrics to build

duration,  job  logs  and  system  performance  but  they  rarely  provide  insight  into

carbon  footprint  or  sustainability  metrics.  Also,  there  is  limited  experimental

research  comparing  these  sustainability  metrics  and  environmental  impact  of

different

CI/CD

platforms

using

identical

workloads.

Also the problem with past studies that they compared these platforms on different,

unknown  hardware.  This  makes  it  impossible  to  know  if  the  differences  in

emissions came from the platforms themselves or just the hardware they ran on. No

one has actually isolated how much energy the orchestration agent uses by testing

all three platforms on the exact same equipment.

In the absence of clear data or benchmarks, developers can’t build greener CI/CD

pipelines. Evaluating the carbon footprint of these workflows is essential to identify

the strategies for enhancing their environmental sustainability and make automated

development more sustainable.

1.3. Objectives of the Project

•  To set up self hosted runners for GitHub Actions, Gitlab CI and Bitbucket

Pipelines on identical virtual machines.

•  To use kernal level tracking tools to measure the exact energy used by each

pipeline process.

•  To  compare  the  energy  overhead  of  each  platform’s  orchestration  agent

under the exact same hardware conditions.

•  To find out which specific pipeline stages cause the highest energy use and

carbon emissions.

•  To create practical, data driven recommendations for picking and setting up

energy efficient CI/CD pipelines.

1.4. Research Questions

•  How does the choice of CI/CD platform actually change energy use when

we run them on the exact same hardware?

•  How  much  extra  energy  does  each  platform’s  runner  agent  waste  just  by

staying active on identical equipment?

•  Which  specific  stages  of  a  pipeline  drain  the  most  power  across  these

different platforms?

•  How can developers  use real data to  pick  a platform that actively  lowers

their CI/CD carbon footprint?

1.5. Scope of the research

This research focuses on evaluating the energy consumption and carbon overhead

of  CI/CD  orchestration  agents  across  three  major  platforms  including  Github

Actions, GitLab Ci and Bitbucket Pipelines. We use exact same virtual machine to

setup all the three platforms as self hosted runners which make it a fair test. We will

use  tools  like  scaphandre  to  track  the  process  level  energy  consumption.  If  VM

environment blocks hardware level power extraction we will move to Codecarbon

as a backup tool.

We will focus on most important stages of building and testing stages in a pipeline

because these stags are the most power heavy stages of a pipeline. We plan to run

the pipeline minimum 30 times for each platform to get more valid statistically valid

data.

We will have solid benchmark dataset, a statistical breakdown of how the platforms

compare and a real time dashboard that shows energy and carbon metrics for each

pipeline  stage.  Finally  we  can  use  these  data  to  give  practical  advice  on  how  to

configure sustainability CI/CD pipelines.

2. Literature Review

2.1. Findings by other researchers

Recent studies on sustainable software engineering have increasingly focused on

the environmental impact of Continuous Integration and Continuous Deployment

(CI/CD)  pipelines.  DevOps  practices  are  continually  improving,  and  CI/CD

pipelines are increasingly used to automate build, test, and deployment processes.

However,  their  impact  on  energy  consumption  and  emissions  has  only  recently

gained attention.

A large-scale study on the environmental impact of CI/CD pipelines was carried out

by Saavedra et al. (2025). The authors used 2.23 million workflow executions on

18,683 public repositories on GitHub Actions to estimate the carbon emissions on

the GitHub Actions platform. The authors found that if all GitHub Actions were

used for CI/CD pipelines in 2024, it may have resulted in 456.9 million tons of CO2

equivalent  (MTCO2e)  with  a  possible  range  between  150  MTCO2e  and  995

MTCO2e.  The  authors  stated  that  CI/CD  service  providers  do  not  disclose

information on energy consumption or emissions. Thus, researchers are forced to

use  indirect  methods  for  estimation.  However,  the  authors  only  focused  on  the

GitHub Actions platform and suggested that future studies need to be conducted on

other platforms as well.

Likewise,  Aneggi  et  al.  (2026)  suggested  the  idea  of  an  energy-aware  CI/CD

pipeline  (PPTAMη),  which  is  implemented  using  the  GitLab  CI  tool.  The  study

included  the  integration  of  real-time  power  and  energy  measurements  with  the

CI/CD  pipeline.  The  study  revealed  that  the  CI/CD  pipeline  rarely  considers  the

energy  consumption  aspect  because  the  majority  of  the  DevOps  tools  give  more

importance  to  the  performance  and  reliability  of  the  application.  The  findings

indicate  the  impact  of  pipeline  configuration  on  the  energy  consumption  of  the

CI/CD  pipeline.  The  study  suggested  the  implementation  of  the  CI/CD  pipeline

using the GitLab CI tool. The study required the implementation of specific tools

for  the  purpose  of  the  study  therefore,  the  study  is  not  helpful  for  the  general

developer community.

In addition to the above mentioned study, the study conducted by Berger-Levrault

(2024) is based on the experimentation carried out using the Eco CI tool. The study

models. Also other tools like CodeCarbon and GreenFrame exists and give general

CI/CD pipelines. Additionally GitHub also introduced sustainable category in its

marketplace  to  promote  Green  and  Environmentally  conscious  development

practices.

However, despite the above developments, research on the sustainability of CI/CD

has been limited. Most critical limitation is these studies are these comparisons run

on  platforms  which  running  on  different,  unknown  hardware  configurations.  No

study has isolated the CI/CD orchestration agent’s energy overhead by running on

identical hardware. This hardware bias means observations differences in existing

studies  cannot  be  directly  say  whether  difference  comes  from  the  platforms

themselves or just different hardware they ran on.

2.2. The research gap

Although recent research shows the environmental impact of CI/CD pipelines and

introduced  tools  and  frameworks  for  estimating,  there  are  still  limitations

remaining.  The  existing  research  is  mostly  platform  specific  which  focuses

primarily on GitHub Action or Gitlab CI without giving platform comparisons. As

highlighted by Saavedra et al. (2025), there is a need for studies that comparison of

carbon emissions across different CI/CD platforms under consistent conditions.

A key yet often ignored problem is that in current studies the platforms ae assessed

using different and unknown cloud hosted setups. In Berger Levrault’s 2024 study

comparing GitHub Actions and GitLab CI, each tool was tested under distinct cloud

infrastructure  with  unique  hardware.  Because  of  this  Its  impossible  to  tell  if  the

energy  difference  noted  are  due  to  platform’s  software  or  simply  the  varying

hardware configurations. No study to date has gotten around this issue by testing

everything on identical hardware at the same time by using self hosted runners.

Many  of  the  existing  approaches  to  energy  measurement  rely  on  complex

instruments  or  specialized  hardware.  And  there’s  still  not  a  standard  way  for

measuring and comparing emissions from CI/CD pipelines, especially when those

pipelines  are  deployed  in  the  cloud.  This  study  address  this  issue  by  using

scaphandre an accessible open source tool that extracts process level energy data

differently from the linux kernel without requiring specialized hardware.

Another main gap is the absence of research on platforms like Bitbucket pipelines

which are widely used in the modern software industry. Despite their widespread

use of the industry there is little information of their environmental impact, leaving

developers without clear benchmarks when considering sustainability.

This highlights the need for practical, experimental studies that isolates the CI/CD

orchestration  agent  as  the  sole  variable  by  running  all  platforms  on  identical

hardware using self hosted runners. This study aims to address this gap by testing

GitHub  Actions,  GitLab  CI  and  Bitbucket  Pipelines  runner  agents  running  on  a

standardized virtual machine. This generate the first data set to compare the energy

usage of different self hosted runner agents across different platforms. We can use

this data to provide actionable insights for sustainable CI/CD platform selection.

2.3. Chapter conclusion

This  chapter  presents  some  recent  findings  in  scientific  literature  regarding  the

impact  of  CI/CD  pipelines  on  the  environment  and  approaches  to  sustainable

DevOps. This research shows that CI/CD pipelines consume electricity, leading to

carbon dioxide emission, while software developers remain unaware of such issues.

A considerable number of studies have provided various approaches to estimating

such an impact, which mostly focused on particular CI/CD platforms.

Nevertheless, there is a significant need for comparative and applicable research,

which assesses CI/CD platforms based on similar workloads using simple tools. It

becomes  critical  because  developers  should  have  relevant  information  when

choosing a proper approach to sustainable software development. The methodology

used during this research will be presented in the next chapter.

3. Methodology

This research involves an experimental approach to evaluate and compare the energy

consumption and carbon overhead of CI/CD orchestration agents  across  three major

DevOps platforms which are GitHub Actions, GitLab CI and Bitbucket Pipelines. All

three  platforms  will  be  configured  to  run  self  hosted  agent  runners  on  a  single

standardized  virtual  machine,  ensuring  that  hardware  remains  a  controlled  constant

across  all  experiments  and  making  the  orchestration  agent  the  only  independent

variable.

We’ll use a standardized Ubuntu linux virtual machine as the shared environment for

all platforms. This VM will have runner agents for each platform, but only active one

during any  experiment.  The VM specifications including CPU,  memory and storage

will  be  documented  to  ensure  the  same  experiment  can  be  reproduced  by  future

researchers.

We  plan  to  use  CodecCarbon  to  get  the  energy  messurements.  It’s  a  tool  that  can

measure CPU utilization at the process level and apply hardware TDP based energy

model. This method selected because we cant use intel RAPL interfaces to measure the

hardware  level  power  in  most  virtual  machines.  CodeCarbon  CPU  utilization  based

estimation is used in most peer reviewed research and operates reliably within cloud

environments.

Then  the  collected  data  will  be  analyzed  and  get  descriptive  statistics  like  mean,

standard deviation, minimum and maximum energy consumption per platform. A stage

level  breakdown  will  identify  which  pipeline  stages  contribute  most  energy

consumption across platforms.

A simple web application will act as a standardized workload for all platforms. Identical

CI/CD  pipelines  will  be  deployed  for  each  of  these  platforms,  which  would  include

stages like build and test, and these pipelines would  be run multiple times to  gather

data.
