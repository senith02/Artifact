## 6b. The RQ2 verdict at the decision altitude (S5, written at the P3-T3 gate)

> Authored text, added after the run. It introduces **no new number**: every figure is copied from
> §§1–6 above. It is stated at the strength the CIs support.

**Verdict.** Under the predeclared pipeline as frozen, **the evidence-derived policy realises no
decision value from commit-level SE characteristics beyond a commit-time duration estimate**. The
policy admitted no SE
family on calibration evidence, so ⑤ uses none, and ⑤ ≡ ④b. Against ④b the area between frontiers is
**+0.0000 [+0.0000, +0.0000]**, no matched point counts on either axis, and the §A1.7 condition fails
at ×0.5, ×1 and ×2. The §A1.9 rule, that ⑤ must beat both ④a and ④b, therefore fails at every floor.

**What that verdict is, and is not.**

- It is a null **by construction** of a pre-registered pipeline, not an independent head-to-head test
  of an SE-informed policy on test data. No SE-informed policy was ever frozen, so none could be
  tested. The decision altitude therefore confirms the calibration-stage outcome; it adds no new
  evidence about SE characteristics beyond it.
- It does **not** settle F1's decision-level value. F1 cleared the model-level floor on test (P3-T1),
  but by author decision no post-hoc F1 arm was built (DL-028 §1), so its effect on the carbon/TTFF
  frontier is **untested**.
- §A1.13 still applies. The carbon channel cannot carry SE value by construction; any SE value would
  have had to appear as lower TTFF p95 at matched carbon, and the frozen spec never gave it the chance.

**Secondary finding: the form of the duration control matters.** ⑤ vs ④a is a comparison between two
**duration** controls, the project-history prior (④b) and the commit-feature regressor (④a). It is not
evidence about SE characteristics as a decision input, although ④a's inputs are the 28 commit-time
features.

- ④b's frontier dominates ④a's. The area is **+2.4308 [+1.7358, +2.5684]** pp × h, and the §A1.7
  condition holds at ×0.5, ×1 and ×2. It holds on the carbon axis (three matched points, 1.29–1.63%
  saved, each ≥ 52% lower TTFF p95 with CIs above 0) and at two TTFF points (3.33 h and 4.98 h).
- The advantage is **concentrated at low-to-mid carbon savings**. Near the frozen operating point
  (≈ 2.48% saved), the matched difference is +0.0360 [−1.7830, +0.7492] h, which is indistinguishable
  from 0. At the frozen point itself, ⑤ − ④a TTFF p95 was −0.18 [−0.62, +0.16] h (P3-T2).
- It is **not uniform across projects**. In the low failure-rate band, ④a's frontier is better (area
  −2.6750 [−4.2721, −1.3844]). In the mid and high bands, ④b's is better (+1.5637 [+0.9861, +1.9189]
  and +2.1981 [+1.8266, +2.5272]).

**Oracle bound (§A1.10 — oracle, unrealizable in deployment).** Perfect knowledge of each build's
duration would move the duration-only frontier outward: oracle vs ④b area **+1.9290 [+1.7625,
+2.3492]** pp × h, condition met at every floor. Estimator error in ④b therefore leaves measurable
headroom, and a better *duration* estimate is the lever this evidence points to. No such arm is
deployable. It bounds the headroom, and it is not a strategy.

**Bounds carried into P3-T5 and P5:** one grid profile and one energy model (P3-T4's sweeps test
both); the frontier is 30 grid points with piecewise-linear interpolation; hour-granular latency;
some matched-point CIs are wide and skewed where frontiers kink (e.g. 1.6316% saved:
+3.5062 [+0.9343, +3.5772]); and 170 test projects.
