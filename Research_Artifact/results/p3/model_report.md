## 7. Context against the literature (S4) — context, not comparability

Mhalla & Saied (2024) report AUC ≈ 0.90 for CI-*skip* detection (spec §2.1; eval_protocol §5). That is a different task (skip-worthiness, not failure), different data and a different label, and it uses no duration control. The ROC-AUCs in §2 are therefore **not comparable** to it and no claim of being better or worse is made. The comparison this study rests on is internal and paired: every SE arm against `{d̂}` on the same test builds.


## 8. What this report does and does not establish

- It is the **model-altitude** RQ1/RQ2 evidence on held-out projects. The decision-altitude RQ2 test (④ vs ⑤ frontiers) is P3-T3's; the strategy comparison is P3-T2's.
- Effect sizes lead (§A1.11): at this N a CI excluding 0 is nearly free, so 'material' is reserved for the §A1.7 floor.
- Bounds carried from P1: F2 is structurally weakened (DL-016), two textbook JIT predictors are absent (DL-015), one algorithm and one search budget were used for the family arms (DL-018/DL-019), and `d̂` is a *predicted* quantity, so the question answered is value beyond *predictable* duration.

## 10. The model-level RQ1 / RQ2 answer (written at the P3-T1 gate)

> Authored text, added after the run. It introduces **no new number**: every figure is copied from
> §§1–6 above or from `replication_table.md`.

**RQ2, model altitude: the calibration-split null does not replicate in full on test.** One family,
**F1 (change size & diffusion)**, clears the §A1.7 model-level floor on the held-out projects.
Its ΔPR-AUC vs `{d̂}` is **+0.013313 [+0.011924, +0.014733]**, with ΔROC-AUC +0.014252 and ΔBrier
−0.002518, all favourable with CIs excluding 0. On the calibration split the same frozen arm was
significantly *worse* than the control (−0.003678 [−0.005310, −0.001958]). The other five families are
rejected on test as on calibration: four with CIs entirely below 0 (F2, F3, F4, F5), and F6 because its
positive ΔPR-AUC (+0.002257) is below the floor.

Three qualifications bound how strongly this can be stated:

1. **It is not stable across the predeclared floor sweep.** F1 is admitted at ×0.5 and ×1 but not at
   ×2 (+0.02), so the admitted set is {F1}, {F1}, ∅ (`stable: False`). §A1.7 treats a stable set as
   the stronger finding; this one is floor-sensitive. Its point estimate is 1.33× the floor.
2. **It is a sign flip between two project-disjoint splits.** Calibration and test are different
   projects with different base rates (28.5492% vs 25.0359% failure) and duration tails (DL-017). The
   evidence therefore says F1's increment over `d̂` is **heterogeneous across projects**, not that it
   is reliably positive. Neither split alone establishes it.
3. **Model-level admission is only half of §A1.7.** Entering the policy also requires frontier
   dominance at the decision altitude (§A1.5), and the policy is **frozen on calibration evidence**
   (`policy_spec.yaml`, duration-only). This test result does not, and under "fit before you look"
   cannot, change the frozen policy. Under the frozen spec ⑤ ≡ ④b, so P3-T3's frontier test has no F1
   arm to evaluate unless one is added. That would be post-hoc and needs a decision-log entry.

**The full SE arm still does not beat the control** on test for any algorithm. ΔPR-AUC is −0.007864
(XGBoost), −0.020635 (logistic regression) and −0.003347 (random forest), all with CIs below 0. That
replicates the P1-T5 calibration pattern: stacking all 28 features loses information relative to `d̂`
even where one family alone adds some.

**RQ1 (which SE characteristics carry decision information).** At the model altitude, only change size
and diffusion (F1) shows a positive increment over the duration control on held-out projects, and it
does not replicate from calibration. Test activity (F3), project history (F4) and developer/team (F5)
are consistently harmful as additions to `d̂` on both splits. Inside the full arm, SHAP ranks are
essentially identical between the splits (Spearman 0.9960, top-10 overlap 10/10). The *attribution*
structure is stable even though the *incremental value* of F1 is not.

**Calibration and discrimination, held out.** Every arm's test PR-AUC is below its calibration value
(for example `xgboost:control` 0.390357 → 0.294228), and PR-AUC depends on prevalence, which fell from
28.55% to 25.04%. ROC-AUC, which does not, also fell (0.650712 → 0.575774 for `xgboost:control`). The
test ECE of 0.030–0.055 is the first out-of-sample calibration measurement; the calibration-split ECE
of ≈ 0 was in-sample by construction. τ is mis-centred as predicted, with predicted-positive rates at
τ of 0.54–0.98 against a base rate of 0.25.

**The duration control holds up on test.** ④b's log1p MAE is 0.5717 against 0.6010 on calibration, and
its Spearman ρ is 0.8199 against 0.8691; it remains far better than ④a (0.9868, ρ 0.5128). The
trailing-50 sensitivity again beats the frozen expanding primary (0.3108, ρ 0.9008), as it did on
calibration; the primary is unchanged per its predeclaration. The ④a Ridge reference's MAE in seconds
(498,081.1) is dominated by a few extreme back-transformed predictions (its median AE is 347.8 s). It
is a reference form, never a control.
