> Authored text, added after the run. It introduces **no new number**; every figure is in §3–§5 above.
> RQ4's verdicts are P3-T5's and RQ2's decision-level verdict is P3-T3's. This section only
> describes what the table shows.

- **The frozen policy saves carbon against static execution at a bounded delay.** ⑤ is
  −70.7 [−78.5, −64.9] gCO₂e per 1,000 builds against ① (−2.480%). It defers 7.92% of builds for at
  most 24 h, adding 1.017 h of mean latency across all builds and +12.21 h to failed-build TTFF p95.
- **Blanket carbon-aware scheduling saves more carbon at a much larger feedback cost.** ② saves
  4.681% against ①, but ⑤'s TTFF p95 is 113.13 h lower [−116.02, −110.91], at +62.7 gCO₂e per 1,000
  builds. Its deferred builds wait up to a week (latency p95 153.00 h).
- **Duration selectivity is what separates ⑤ from ③.** ③ defers every eligible build for 24 h. At
  +9.6 gCO₂e per 1,000 builds, ⑤ cuts TTFF p95 by 5.93 h and mean latency by 1.424 h: it gives up a
  little carbon to avoid delaying short builds.
- **④a and ④b at the same point are nearly indistinguishable.** ⑤ (≡ ④b) − ④a is −2.0 gCO₂e per
  1,000 builds, and its TTFF p95 CI spans 0 (−0.18 [−0.62, +0.16]). The frontier comparison at
  matched points is P3-T3's.
- **⑥'s carbon comes from not running builds.** At τ = 0.30 it saves 14.532% but misses 6,689
  failures (recall 0.8074). At every smaller positive τ it saves less than ⑤ and still misses failures; at τ = 0 it is ①.
- **Gate safety holds on the test trace.** No non-deferrable build was deferred or skipped in
  14,146,686 records. As DL-020 §6 states, that shows consistency with the rule, not the rule's
  correctness.
